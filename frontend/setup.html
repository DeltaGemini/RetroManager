Where RetroArch keeps saves

ScreenScraper (optional)

RetroManager fetches each game's description, box art, screenshots and videos from sites that keep them. ScreenScraper has the most for old consoles, and asks every program that uses it to identify itself, so it needs two things from you.

  1. An account of your own. Register at screenscraper.fr (free; the site is in French with an English option at the top). That gives you a user name and password.
  2. Developer credentials. Signed in there, open the site's forum and ask for API access for a program: say it's for your own copy of RetroManager, a game library manager. ScreenScraper's team reply with a developer ID and password. It's done by hand, so it can take a few days.
  3. Enter them below. The developer pair is what turns ScreenScraper on; your user name and password raise how much you may fetch each day.

They're kept in RetroManager's own folder on this machine and sent only to ScreenScraper. You can add or change them later in Tools › Settings, where the other sources (IGDB, RetroAchievements, SteamGridDB, TheGamesDB, LaunchBox) are too.

Without ScreenScraper, RetroManager still works: games are listed and played, and pictures can come from libretro's thumbnails, GameTDB and Steam, which ask for nothing.

Who can change things (recommended)

As it is, anyone who can reach RetroManager on your network can edit the library, delete games and change settings. Turn on login, and each person picks themselves when they open it: you're the admin, with a PIN, and people you add later can play and keep their own lists but not change the library.

Names, PINs and who's an admin can be changed later in Tools › Profiles and login, where login can be switched off again too.

That's it

  1. Add games: ➕ Add games at the top of every page takes files from this device, or put them in the games folder, one folder per system.
  2. Fetch their details: open a system and press Scan, then Bulk to scrape what's missing.
  3. More than one person? Tools › Profiles and login gives each their own favourites and a sign-in, and their own saves where saves are kept in folders of their own.
  4. On a phone: the Android app plays the library in RetroArch; the "Android app" page of the documentation (docs/phone.md) has it.
